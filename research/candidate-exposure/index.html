Candidate Exposure and Abstention in Entity Linking

Holding the query fixed, we tested whether the entities shown to a model change its willingness to abstain.

TAE poster and expanded EvoRobust paper · NeurIPS 2026 workshops

Read the TAE paper ↗

Changing the candidate list

In selective entity linking, a system should abstain when it finds no supported entity. The TAE study held each query fixed and compared retrieved candidates with alternatives including random candidates.

For GPT-OSS 20B on benchmark-NIL queries, the retrieved lists increased entity selection by 6.3 percentage points on Beauty and 10.7 on ESCI relative to random lists.

Testing the effect beyond the first setup

The EvoRobust study added hard negatives matched for lexical and semantic similarity, list-size controls, and transfer tests on Wikipedia and French and German historical news. Compared with those hard negatives, retrieved candidates raised entity selection on benchmark-NIL queries by 5.0 percentage points on Beauty and 9.7 on ESCI.

We also tested Structured Support Verification. On a separate held-out product-search test across three verifier models, it retained 87.7–92.0% of gold proposals and rejected 85.3–86.3% of plausible same-query alternatives.
